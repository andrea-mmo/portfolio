The brief

Milagros, the owner, has run her salon for more than four decades and has a loyal clientele and a 4.7 rating on Google, but no website. New customers searching for a hairdresser nearby had nowhere to see what she offers or how to book.

She needed something simple that works for her: no booking software to learn, no online shop to manage, and a tone that sounds like her.

The approach

I treated it as a marketing problem first and a design problem second. The site has one job: get a visitor to send a WhatsApp message or call. Everything on the page either builds trust or makes that step easier.

The copy is written in first person, in Milagros's own voice, because customers are choosing a person, not a brand.

Key decisions

  1. One clear conversion goal

    WhatsApp is how her customers already talk to her, so it is the primary action everywhere: in the sticky header, the hero, the services section and the footer. A phone number sits beside it for those who prefer to call.

  2. Trust before the ask

    Real Google reviews, the “+40 years” seal and a personal story do the persuading. The rating and number of reviews link straight to Google so visitors can check them.

  3. Selling products without a shop

    The salon's retail products are presented like a small catalogue. Each one opens WhatsApp with a pre-written message naming the product, giving her e-commerce style enquiries with nothing to maintain.

  4. Built to be measured

    Every button is tracked as its own event by position (header, hero, services, each product, contact) with Umami, a cookieless analytics tool, so we can see which sections actually drive enquiries and improve from there.

  5. Easy to find, good to share

    Its own domain, descriptive titles, address, opening hours, an embedded map and semantic HTML to support her Google Business profile, plus a branded preview card when the link is shared on WhatsApp or social media.

  6. Mobile first, accessible, compliant

    Designed for phones first and adapted to tablet and desktop. Large text, strong contrast, keyboard-friendly carousels that respect reduced-motion settings, plus legal, privacy and cookie pages.

Feature spotlight

The hair quiz: a lead magnet that recommends, sells and spreads

After launch I added a one-minute quiz, “¿Qué necesita tu pelo?” (What does your hair need?). Four simple questions lead to a personal result, turning a casual visitor into an enquiry that already says what they need.

  • 1. A low-effort promise
  • 2. One tap per question
  • 3. A result worth sharing

  • Personal recommendations

    Each answer scores against five possible outcomes. The result shows the main need, a second one when it applies, the salon's own products for home and the right treatment in the salon.

  • Two ways to convert

    “Book an appointment” opens WhatsApp with a message that already names the treatment. A second button sends the result itself, so Milagros knows what the customer needs before she replies.

  • Made to be shared

    The result becomes a story-sized image, generated in the browser and passed to the phone's share menu. A “send it to a friend” link with a ready-written message brings new visitors in.

  • Placed where doubt appears

    The quiz is offered in the hero, next to “not sure what you need?” in services, and above the products, exactly where a visitor might hesitate.

  • Measured as a funnel

    Starts, each result type, shares, booking clicks and retakes are tracked as events, showing which hair concerns are most common and which entry point works best.

Beyond the website

Instagram and the salon's online presence

A website is only one of the places customers look. I also set up and now run the rest of the salon's presence online, so everything speaks with the same voice.

  • Instagram, managed end to end

    I created the salon's account, @peluqueriamorenovlc, launched in October 2026, and I manage all of it: photos, captions, content planning, stories and replies to messages.

  • Web and social working together

    The quiz gives the account a steady source of content: each customer who posts their result and tags the salon introduces it to new people nearby.

  • A professional point of contact

    I set up a dedicated business email for the salon, shown on the site and its legal pages, so enquiries have a proper home alongside WhatsApp and phone.

The brief

The company needs a website, and the first decision is the biggest one: how it should look and who it should speak to first, whether households, businesses or the local neighbourhood.

Asking “what style do you like?” rarely gets a useful answer, so I gave them something real to react to instead.

The approach

Each concept is a different positioning, not just a different colour palette. It changes who the page talks to, what it asks the visitor to do and which sections it needs to get there.

All six are built as working, responsive pages and gathered on one presentation page that explains, in plain language, what each one conveys, who it suits and what it includes.

  • A decision the client can make alone

    The presentation page avoids design jargon. Every card answers the same three questions, so the options can be compared side by side without me in the room.

  • Seen the way customers will see it

    Each proposal opens in a phone preview from the desktop, and directly in its mobile version when the link is opened on a phone.

  • Open to mixing

    The concepts share the same building blocks, so the style of one can be combined with the sections of another.

  • What comes next

    Once a direction is chosen, I'll replace the sample copy, prices and photos with the company's own and prepare the site for launch.
