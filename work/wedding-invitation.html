Online wedding invitation

An invitation that opens like an envelope on the guest's phone, with everything they need to know and a short form to reply, saved in a private spreadsheet for the couple.

The brief

The couple wanted something more personal than a PDF or a WhatsApp image: a page guests could open from a QR code on the printed invitation, with the day's details, the dress code, the gift information and a way to say whether they are coming.

The hard part is the replies. Each guest might bring a partner or children, have allergies, need a bus seat or want to suggest a song, and the couple has to turn all of that into a headcount and a catering list.

The approach

I started from the paper invitation they had chosen, with its watercolour hydrangeas, green envelope and gold seal, and built the website as the same object: you open the envelope first, then read the card.

I made seven different design directions, from editorial and minimal to illustrated, so the couple could react to real pages. They chose the envelope. Then I focused on making the reply form something anyone can finish quickly on a phone.

Key decisions

  1. The envelope sets the tone

    The first screen is a sealed envelope that opens as the guest slides a finger up, and the page's palette, hydrangeas and lettering all come from the printed invitation. It is the one moment of surprise, and it only happens once.

  2. Everything a guest asks, in one place

    A countdown, the ceremony and the celebration with directions in one tap, an “add to calendar” file with reminders a week, a day and three hours before, the dress code with the colours to avoid shown as swatches, and the bus pick-up point.

  3. One question per screen

    The reply form is a short wizard. It shows only the steps that apply: companions and children appear after a “yes”, allergies are asked per person, and the bus questions show their follow-ups only when needed. The last step summarises the answers before sending.

  4. Three languages and a night mode

    A small ES · CA · EN selector switches the whole page, including the form, error messages, calendar reminders and the date on the cover. It remembers the choice and starts in the visitor's browser language. A sun and moon button adds a night mode, with a dark green palette and soft light behind the watercolour flowers so they do not look dull.

  5. Replies the couple can actually use

    Answers are saved to a private Google Sheet through a small Apps Script, with separate tabs for replies, people, guests and a summary of totals. It checks each invitation's maximum number of people, flags anything to review, and lets guests change their answer with the same name until the deadline.

  6. Money talk, kept discreet

    The gift section is a gentle sentence and a button. The account number stays hidden until the guest taps to reveal it, and can then be copied in one tap. In this demo the number is obviously fake.

  7. Private by design

    The page is hidden from search engines, has no analytics and no tracking, and replies are readable only by whoever has access to the sheet. Names, dates, venues and photos live in one settings file, so changing them never means touching the code.

Behind the scenes

Details that make it feel finished

  • Seven directions, one chosen

    Envelope, watercolour, boarding pass, illustrated, classic, editorial and arches. Showing real pages beat asking what style they liked.

  • Eight gallery styles

    Carousel, mosaic, polaroid, arches, frames and more, switched with one setting so the couple could see their own photos in each before deciding.

  • Light and quick

    Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework, compressed images, so the page opens fast on a guest's phone. Motion is switched off for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

  • Printed and online, together

    I prepared the QR code for the printed invitation and the files for publishing, so the paper and the page lead to the same place.
